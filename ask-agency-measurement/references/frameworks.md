# Defining agency (Step 1)

Use this to ask sharp diagnostic questions and recommend answers. The frameworks below are examples, not a menu — find a better-fitting one when the case calls for it.

## Diagnostic questions (ask one at a time, recommend an answer)
- **Whose agency, over what?** Which users, and agency toward which end (a health decision, a learning goal, a livelihood, civic voice)?
- **Which form?** Toward the end goal, toward the tool itself (can they understand, question, adapt it), or both?
- **Which manifestation?** Felt internally (perceived control, self-efficacy) or expressed externally (choices, actions)?
- **What's the before/after in the user's own life** — not on the platform?
- **What would agency going *down* look like** (dependence, narrowed choices, less reflection)?
- **What constraints decide whether agency can be exercised** (norms, institutions, resources, gatekeepers)?

A framework that can't express the answers to these is the wrong one.

## Example frameworks (one-liners)
- **Bandura (SCT):** four internal processes — intentionality, forethought, self-reactiveness, self-reflectiveness. Good when you can observe a process.
- **Sen (capability approach):** real freedom to act on what one values; foregrounds external affordances and constraints; separates ability from autonomy. Good where structure dominates.
- **Jigsaw's five dimensions:** instrumental, cognitive, affective, relational, structural. Good for naming which kind of agency an AI feature helps or erodes.
- **Integrative/systems framings:** aspiration + internal affordances + external affordances + navigation as a loop over time.

Most plans borrow from more than one — just be explicit about which construct each measure captures.

## Go beyond when
The user has a specific population (women/girls, youth, farmers, patients), a domain-specific agency (political, moral, educational, financial), a non-Western tradition, or needs to detect erosion. Then research a fitting framework (see `research-queries.md`).
